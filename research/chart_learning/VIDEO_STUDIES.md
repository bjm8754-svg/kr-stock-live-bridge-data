### Analytical interpretation — original hypotheses, not canon

1. **Capacity-adjusted trade planning:** Construct a liquidity and fill-risk budget BEFORE setting size; an entry's theoretical R/R is not economic R/R if intended order size overwhelms bid/ask depth. Test separately across order amounts, spreads, traded value and volatility. Do not elevate 1–2% slippage anecdote to a constant.
2. **Confluence is conditional, not a vote:** Bullish candle or chart breakout without regime/catalyst/participation context can be a weak opportunity. However, investor-flow reporting delays, false narratives and late-stage thematic chasing may render additional filters harmful. Compare simpler price/volume/structure versus expanded context on unseen cases instead of imposing arbitrary numeric scores.
3. **Abstention is a positive decision when opportunity is weak**, but 'stop after any morning profit' is a discipline heuristic for intraday day trading, not a universal exit/time rule for swing holdings. A pre-existing swing thesis, pending limit and structural stop remain independent of a daypart.
4. **Concentration has a trade-off:** 3–4 active names can aid monitoring, but overly concentrated correlated positions amplify drawdown. Do not turn an individual trader's preferred count into a universal limit; choose risk budget, correlated exposures and management capacity rationally.
5. **Narrative reversal risk:** The high-drawdown recovery anecdote does not establish correct risk management, because a similar position might not recover. Require up-front invalidation, worst-case gap loss and risk-of-ruin assessment rather than calling survival success.
6. **Swing scaling and rare market regimes:** hypothesis that patient accumulation in genuinely strong trends can be more capacity-efficient than high-frequency scalping, but must compare costs, risk, missed signals, regime instability and longer thesis horizons.

- On full-cohort, pre-outcome snapshots, does adding flow/catalyst/liquidity/regime filters improve expected risk-adjusted economic outcomes relative to a simpler price/volume/time structural baseline? How many candidates are wrongly rejected (missed winners)?
