## B. Execution-mode conflict exposed by comparing raw user text with diagrams

The full canonical appendix [raw text pp.40–43, '왜 도달하면 바로 매수하는가'] advocates **a preidentified trend-connector price and immediate buy on touch**, with structural close invalidation, because waiting for a rebound worsens average entry.

Yet slides #045/#051/#052/#056/#058 and #064–081 emphasize the **reaction, reclaim, and subsequent role acceptance** to avoid a false floor or false breakout. These sources do not instruct the exact same information set. They express two distinct conditional choices:

| Source-specific route | What can exist BEFORE touch | What must be observed BEFORE asserting the route occurred | Failure and opportunity cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0 preplanned trend-connector buy** (canonical raw pp.40–43) | Prior impulse → pause → renewed movement at **known cutoff**, marked connector close plus wick region, order conditions precommitted | It is a *hypothesis of future support*, **not** already validated defense. Actual subsequent touch/fill only when future arrives | Falls through connector at order fill; close can gap far below, slippage/overnight gap and risk cap needed; an exact connector may be chosen after seeing future (look-ahead). |
| **P1 box-underthrow / spring-reclaim buy** (#052/#056/#058) | Old box L and qualified completed-candle rule | Price attempted below old L **and could not remain below**, then re-entered accepted range. A simple first undercut does NOT qualify | Re-entry after price has run higher; second underthrow and later failure; may never pull back. |
| **P2 close-confirmed upper role-flip then pullback** (#064/#066/#068/#074/#078–081) | Pre-existing U and C_box, old box established without seeing later result | Qualified completed close above U; subsequent pullback/role acceptance before declaring defended support | Shallow U not revisited, deep C_box fill may coincide with structural deterioration; two planned limits can only be staged using *source-consistent as-of* prerequisites. |

A model that waits for a spring **cannot retrospectively claim the cheaper pre-spring order** at 9,800. A model using P0 cannot retrospectively claim it first observed the later rebound. **Do not compare a hindsight-filled order from mode P0 with confirmation in P1/P2 without information-time control.** P0, P1 and P2 may eventually prove useful, but superiority is unverified. No actual orders or prices are recommended here.

### Comparing the two teachers *without falsely equating their methods*

The curated slide teacher prioritizes **observable price anchors and close-based role assignment**, with RSI relegated to secondary context. The Telegram speaker often begins with **RSI/multitimeframe dissonance**, but then discusses actual upper trendline, supply and follow-on price behavior. These are not necessarily incompatible:

1. **Location/structure first:** mark which instrument, qualified bar duration and source-drawn candidate level (box U/C_box/L, channel upper, prior weekly supply).
2. **Price progress second:** did price actually close or sustain through the relevant resistance? Has the same line been defended after retest? Do not infer a close from a live 01:35 daily chart.
3. **RSI state third:** daily RSI cooling while hourly price is climbing may be a change of slope, averaging window or unfinished daily candle. Define RSI reset/timeframe and **same timestamp** before comparing; RSI 80→50 in the speaker's *example* is not a trade trigger.
4. **No behavioral guarantee:** both sources rely on later market reactions, and the Telegram poster's historical illustration/replay screenshot cannot certify ex-ante execution.
