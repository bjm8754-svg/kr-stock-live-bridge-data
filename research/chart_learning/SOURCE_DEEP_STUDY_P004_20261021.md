## B. Price-level and inclined boundary: seven genuinely different states

| Source | Graphic observation | Methodological consequence |
|---|---|---|
| #012/#013 | Source says chart-reading revolves around locating price where response changes; same geometric line above/below current price is resistance/support as *candidate function*, not permanent name. | A historical level needs a time-qualified **role**, not hard-coded forever `support` because it was once below price. |
| #014 | Seven dimensions include location, repeated trading/response, transfer of roles, temporary nature, strength differences and judgment instead of prediction. | Repeated price encounters are **evidence**, not automatic win statistics; line importance and role strength differ. |
| #015 | Source explicitly extends role logic from **horizontal line to oblique trendline**: below former lower diagonal after failure can produce upper resistance. | A slope needs **at least two author-visible anchors, timeframe, coordinates and point-in-time validity**. Sep08 channel case documented in SRC-DEEP-003 has alternative historical slopes that even reverse above/below verdict. |
| #016/#017 | Price at support may hold, fail, or later revisit broken support from below as resistance. Role-change path matters more than 'buy support' slogan. | Sample educational breakout arrows are alternative narratives, not one executable high-confidence choice. |
| #018/#019 | A **gap** is a discontinuity in traded prices relative to an existing price barrier; standard breakout and gap breakout are variants of boundary crossing. | **Gap height itself** does not determine support-role adoption; a strong gap above a resistance may fail and immediately recross. |
| #020 | Gap over prior resistance → possible later support test at old resistance (e.g. illustrative 10,000), while gap-up price may never retest. | Gap is not a free fill at untraded intermediate prices; opening above old U means hypothetical limit on that crossing bar may **not be filled**. |
| #021 | Gap down through old 10,000 support may make 10,000 new overhead resistance; gap down but remaining **above** established support is a different state. | Do not call every open-price drop below yesterday’s close a structural breakdown; mark the **actual old support boundary**. |
| #022 | Four examples: above-resistance upside gap / still below-resistance upside gap / below-support downside gap / still above-support downside gap. | Classify based on prior barrier and subsequent close/acceptance, not buy the empty region as if liquidity existed there. |

### Slope / gap / RSI role consistency
- The **same previous level** may be candidate support/resistance after the relevant boundary transition, whether horizontal, sloping or jumped by gap.
- For a **sloping drawn level**, later extrapolation is unstable without the endpoints and a fixed coordinate chart; a visible touch alone can be an after-the-fact re-fit. For a *horizontal* box or event close, sourcing remains necessary but coordinate volatility is less acute.
- For a **gap**, the untraded path cannot be treated as executed support trading. Successful re-entry depends on actual future intersection and venue/filled orders, unknown in an annotated screenshot.
- For **RSI**, only interpret after price structure and timeframe. An incomplete candle can revise apparent divergence at its eventual close.
