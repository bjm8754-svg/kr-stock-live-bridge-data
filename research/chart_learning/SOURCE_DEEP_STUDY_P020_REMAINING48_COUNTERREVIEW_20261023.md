## 2. What the48 actually challenge

### A. A boundary's prior origin, current side and demonstrated response are separate facts

**Observed:** #012–015/#023/#025–027/#034–036/#042–045 describe traded-price origins, current position and close-based role changes. #013's summary links price above/below to support/resistance; #039 explicitly shows a high-volume close producing no useful later reaction; #040 rejects “long box automatically strong.”

**Candidate refinement:** location relative to a reference is a structural descriptor and may assign a *candidate role*. It is not yet demonstrated support. Ask separately: why this old price; is the selected completed bar above/below it; did a later retest actually reject/accept; what opposite-side evidence would defeat the claim? This distinction is not dependent on resolving the internal-box term.

**Rivals / falsifiers:** no later response (#039); a large event while the next retest breaks through; a thin, stale sideways range (#040); continued closes back inside after an apparent breakout. A reference may remain historically meaningful while having no executable advantage at the current position.

**Important limit:** #026/#030/#044 explain remembered decisions and accumulated positions. Historical traded volume records transactions, not today's remaining cost distribution or trapped holders. Repeated turnover can recycle the same inventory; buy and sell participation coexist. Supply/defense intent and current holdings remain interpretations unless separately observed. This supports cautious language, not rejecting price/volume/time as useful context.

### B. Gaps describe a crossing of a known boundary; “fill the gap” can hide a losing premise

**Observed:** #018–022 distinguish four source-drawn states: gap above prior resistance, gap up still below resistance, gap below prior support, and gap down still above support. #020 uses old10,000 resistance and10,500 opening; #021 contrasts9,500 below old10,000 support with10,300 still above support. #022 explicitly separates a skipped boundary from an empty area.

**Candidate question:** did the gap cross a pre-existing meaningful boundary, and where did the chosen completed bar settle? A rise still below resistance may face supply; a fall still above support need not be a breakdown. On an adverse gap, returning toward a former support can be a supply test, not a promise of recovery to cost.

**Counterexamples:** an opening cross closes back across the boundary; no retest occurs; a retest fails; a different session/venue defines a different apparent gap. Diagrams do not prove a literal absence of executions across all venues, price-by-volume voids, or an executable opening/stop fill. No fixed “gaps always fill” rule is supported. Missing intermediate trades or unavailable sessions cannot be reconstructed from white screen space.

### E. Cost-basis improvement is not structural repair or a loss cap

**Observed:** #016 shows both a stop branch and a branch adding at a lower support then seeking a rebound exit; #017 distinguishes a new lower-support rebound trade from holding the original broken premise. Edited PDF p3 at13:46–14:49 similarly discusses adding/waiting for a break-even escape after monthly failure; p4 at20:35 supplies a monthly invalidation clock. The PDF preserves an educational explanation, **not independently verified original chart/audio or actual trade records**.

**Conflict to retain:** price-role transition may explain why the old support becomes overhead supply. It does not establish that price will return, that more capital is available, or that adding improves risk-adjusted outcomes. Break-even depends on one's actual fills/size/costs and can move with an add; an old structural level exists independently of the position's average cost. They can coincide but are not the same object.

**Candidate clarification:** distinguish original-thesis invalidation, a separately justified lower-support setup, remaining total-risk capacity, and an optional exit-on-rebound plan. An exit plan conditional on a rebound is incomplete as a loss-limit plan unless it also addresses *no rebound*. A monthly structural clock cannot guarantee an emergency execution price before month-end. No universal add/hold/stop choice or allocation formula is promoted.

**Adverse evidence absent from the lesson:** another gap lower, suspended liquidity, repeated averaging, failure of the new support, or a rebound too small to reach a cost-adjusted exit. Those are counter-scenarios, not events observed in a newly opened market case. L021/C015 record the methodological issue without running a mock trade or calculating fictitious returns.
